Simple Linear Regression

Linear regression with a single explanatory variable.

This part introduces the basics of regression analysis, which we will then use for estimation, comparisons, and prediction.

This part is split into two chapters:

  1. Ordinary Linear Models (regression of continuous outcomes with a central tendency)
  2. Generalized Linear Models (regression of counts, ratios, binary outcomes, and more)

To get started, watch the videos on ordinary linear models.

These are the most information-dense chapters of the website. Take your time to familiarize yourself with the contents. Both chapters end with interactive apps to test your knowledge, and the ultimate goal: A full analysis for you to do in RStudio.

TipWhat level of detail do I need for the exam?

If you are using this book in the context of one of my courses, please note the following:

  • On the exam, you interpret these models from their regression table. Focus on what the intercept and slope mean, and what you can conclude from their p-values and confidence intervals.
  • You judge the assumptions from diagnostic plots. Know what each plot is used for and what clear violations look like.
  • For generalized linear models, know which distribution suits which type of outcome (continuous, counts, ratios, binary), what causes overdispersion, and how to interpret the output of a GLM.
  • You do not need to memorize technical definitions in the summaries, such as the natural parameter and the canonical link function.
